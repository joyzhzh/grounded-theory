# Grounded-theory analysis contract

## Declared method profile

Every study charter must name one operational profile and explain deviations.
Do not silently combine incompatible terminology or procedures. Until that
choice is reviewed, use the conservative label “inductive process study using
grounded-theory techniques.” A Gioia data structure is an optional reporting
surface after analysis, not a substitute for constant comparison or
theoretical sampling.

## Units

- `MANIFESTATION`: exact retained source bytes or authorized trace export.
- `REALIZATION_EPISODE`: an intended outcome and the ordered path to
  acceptance, transformation, substitution, decomposition, postponement,
  abandonment, or unknown.
- `INCIDENT`: one bounded event or source-stated interpretation within an
  episode.
- `CODE`: a close-to-data analytical label attached to one or more incidents.
- `COMPARISON`: an explicit similarity, difference, condition, or consequence
  relation among incidents, codes, episodes, or categories.
- `CATEGORY`: a provisional higher-order construct with properties and
  boundaries.
- `MEMO`: a versioned analytical argument, not evidence.
- `SAMPLING_REQUEST`: a discriminating request generated by a category gap,
  rival, boundary, or negative case.

Episode boundaries must record whether they are source-explicit,
reconstructed, or uncertain.

## Coding

Initial codes stay close to observable action or source language. Prefer
gerunds where they capture process. Do not seed them with candidate theory.
An in-vivo code retains exact wording and locator. A code may be superseded or
retired but never silently edited in place.

## Constant comparison

Record comparisons rather than merely claiming they occurred. At minimum,
compare:

- incident with incident;
- early and late incidents within an episode;
- accepted, compromised, transformed, and abandoned episodes;
- stable and changing specifications;
- similar failures followed by different responses;
- different failures followed by similar responses;
- early and later experience;
- source types, creators, languages, and technological regimes; and
- expected patterns with negative or deviant cases.

Each comparison states the compared IDs, observed similarity or difference,
possible condition, rival explanation, and what evidence would discriminate.

## Category development

A category record states:

- precise definition and what it is not;
- properties and dimensions;
- conditions and consequences;
- supporting codes and incidents;
- counterexamples and negative cases;
- rival explanations;
- changes from its predecessor; and
- confidence basis rather than an ungrounded score.

Interesting language is not support. A category with no incident comparison is
`SENSITIZING_ONLY`, not emerging theory.

## Theoretical sampling

Sampling follows analytical uncertainty. Each request names the proposition or
category gap, target contrast, eligible data/source class, counter-search,
stop rule, and expected discriminating observation. It seeks support,
contradiction, boundary conditions, and rivals—not just more examples.

## Negative cases

For every candidate relationship, search for cases that should violate it.
Revise the relationship when they survive source and custody review. Do not
dismiss deviant cases as noise merely because they complicate the narrative.

## Memos and process theory

Keep descriptive, comparison, methodological, and theoretical memos distinct.
The desired endpoint is a parsimonious process explanation of conditions,
events, interpretations or observable responses, actions, consequences,
adaptation, and alternative pathways. A taxonomy of themes is not the end.

## Stop language

Track whether targeted additions change category properties, relationships, or
boundaries. The tool may recommend `PROVISIONAL_SUFFICIENCY_CANDIDATE`. Only an
independent review plus explicit human methodological judgment can determine
whether a stronger saturation claim is warranted. Blocked, thin, capped, and
positive-tail routes remain unknown.
