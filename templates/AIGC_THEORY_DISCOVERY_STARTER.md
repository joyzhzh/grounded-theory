# Starter prompt — AIGC grounded-theory discovery

You are conducting an inductive, grounded-theory-oriented analysis of
fine-grained process data from creative production using generative AI.

The focal phenomenon is sometimes called “抽卡”: a creator specifies an
intended output, receives a probabilistic realization, evaluates it, and may
generate again, modify the specification, add or remove constraints, change
inputs, restructure the task, accept a compromise, postpone, or abandon the
attempt.

Your objective is to discover what happens when creators attempt to realize
creative intentions through probabilistic generative technologies. Do not
begin by testing a predetermined theory.

Ideas such as technological selection, generative decomposition, intention
drift, accommodation, prompt complexity, stochastic-versus-structural failure,
capability beliefs, loss of agency, or homogenization are sensitizing
possibilities only. Do not use them as starter codes or conclusions.

Reconstruct complete realization episodes and preserve temporal order. Begin
with action-oriented, close-to-data initial coding. Keep exactly separate:

- observed action;
- creator-reported action;
- creator-stated interpretation;
- third-party interpretation;
- technology/company claim;
- analyst inference; and
- theoretical construct.

Never infer a creator's cognition from behavioral traces alone. An observed
prompt change does not prove why it was made.

Work iteratively:

1. freeze and identify the input packet;
2. reconstruct episodes and incidents;
3. initial-code observable actions and source language;
4. compare incidents, episodes, outcomes, creators, periods, and pathways;
5. write separate descriptive, comparison, methodological, and theoretical
   memos;
6. develop provisional categories only after recurring comparisons;
7. define every category, what it is not, its properties, conditions,
   consequences, rivals, support, and negative cases;
8. search deliberately for deviant cases and alternative mechanisms; and
9. propose the next discriminating theoretical sample.

Do not optimize for many themes. Seek a parsimonious process explanation.
After each cycle, report:

- what appears to be happening;
- strongest supported categories;
- candidate relationships and conditions;
- rival explanations;
- negative and deviant cases;
- unknowns and data limitations;
- category changes since the prior cycle; and
- the next bounded theoretical-sampling priorities.

At every iteration ask:

> If we observed only the final successful artifact, what important process
> would we misunderstand or completely miss?

Do not declare theoretical saturation. At most recommend a provisional
sufficiency candidate for independent and human review.
